In short

  • Lunaway shows no advertising, uses no tracker or audience measurement, and sells no data.
  • Browsing the map and computing a route need no account. Until you create one, your favourites, settings and vehicle stay on your device.
  • The account is created when you first contribute or sync your favourites. It asks for no email, no phone number and no password. Once the account exists, your favourite lists are kept with it.
  • Your location is used on the device. It goes to our servers to compute a route, rounded to about 5 km to look for the cheapest fuel around you, and as the position of a place you add where you stand. Routes are neither stored nor logged.
  • Our servers are in Germany. The access logs truncate IP addresses and are erased 14 days after each file is closed.
  • You can delete your account at any time, in the app or on the Delete my account page.

Who is responsible

Lunaway is published by AxiomTeam, the controller of the processing described here. For a question, or to exercise your rights: contact@lunaway.net. The publisher's postal address is in the legal notice.

This policy covers the Lunaway app (Android, iPhone and iPad, macOS, Windows, and the web version at lunaway.net/app), this site, and our servers: api.lunaway.net for data, photos and routes, tiles.lunaway.net for the basemap and the offline maps, status.lunaway.net for the state of our services.

On your device

The app keeps its data in its own storage, which other apps cannot read:

  • The places of the regions you keep on the device (the regions of France and the European countries the app offers), so that the map, the search and the place pages work without a network; the photos, the first page of reviews and the shops and services around the places you opened.
  • The offline maps you download, on phones and tablets.
  • Your own data: your favourite lists, your settings (language, filters, theme, preferred navigation app, route options, guidance settings), your vehicle profile (type, trailer, height, width, length, weight, fuel, consumption), the regions you keep, and the contributions waiting for a connection to be sent (ratings, reviews, prepared photos, new places).
  • An approximate position and the centre of the map's last view, rounded to a tenth of a degree (about ten kilometres), so that the light or dark theme follows sunset, the map opens again where you left it, and the app can suggest your first region.
  • The danger zones and speed cameras of the countries your routes cross, with the list of those countries, so that a guidance started without a network has them.
  • Your account's private key, if you have an account, encrypted by the system's protected storage: a Keystore key on Android, the keychain on iPhone, iPad and Mac, Windows data protection on a PC; in the web app, a key the browser does not let out. It never leaves the device.

The app keeps no route, destination, trip or search. A computed route stays two minutes in memory, to show it again. In the web app, the address of a route's page contains the destination: it enters the browser's history.

To add a photo, or to read your recovery card's code from an image, the app opens the camera or the photo library when you ask, and reads only the image you choose; the code is read on the device. If you save your recovery card's image to your photos, it follows your photo library's settings, online backup included: keep it like a password.

On Android, your device backup, which goes to your Google account, may contain your favourites, settings, vehicle, your account's identifier and the contributions not yet sent, and nothing else from Lunaway: not the account's key, the places or the photos waiting to be sent. The app allows it only when that backup is end-to-end encrypted, which Android offers from version 9 when the screen is locked; Android 7 and 8 do not know this condition and follow their own backup settings. A transfer to a new phone carries the same data. On iPhone and iPad, the iCloud or computer backup holds the same data and the photos waiting to be sent; the account's key, kept in the keychain for this device only, does not move to another device. On a computer, your backups may also contain the app's data, depending on their settings.

In the web app, this data sits in the storage your browser keeps for lunaway.net. Uninstalling the app, or clearing the site's data in the browser, erases all of it.

What goes through our servers

The app connects only to our servers, always over HTTPS. It embeds no third-party service: no advertising, no audience measurement, no crash reporting service, no basemap or font loaded from elsewhere. Only the links you choose to open (OpenStreetMap, your navigation app, the source code) lead to other sites.

With or without an account:

  • Syncing places downloads the regions you keep on the device (France to begin with, and the country where the app last located you, or the one of the device's settings, if it is elsewhere): one file per region, then its changes. The request names the region, never where you are.
  • Offline maps download from tiles.lunaway.net, one file per region. The access log replaces the file's name with a single name for all.
  • The basemap and the shops and services on the map come from tiles.lunaway.net and api.lunaway.net. Each piece of map shown covers an area: the log keeps only its zoom level, never the area.
  • Fuel prices on the map: when the fuel layer shows and the map is close enough, the app asks for the stations of the visible area, widened to a grid of about 5 km, neither stored nor logged.
  • The cheapest fuel around you: when the map is zoomed far out, or shows too many stations to read them all, the app asks for the stations around your position or the map's centre, rounded on the device to about 5 km, neither stored nor logged. Distances are computed on the device. A station's page asks for its price history, with no position at all.
  • Opening a place asks for its reviews and photos, and for the shops and services around the place (the request names the place only). If you have an account, the request for reviews and photos carries your session, to show your own review and leave out the authors you muted; the server keeps from it only the date the session was last used, updated at most once an hour, and the account's day of activity, which counts toward the trust level. Neither the content of these requests nor the address of the photos downloaded is stored or logged.
  • Search for places and towns runs on the device, in the web app as everywhere else: the text searched does not leave the device.
  • Search for shops and services asks our server: the text searched and the map's centre, rounded on the device to about 5 km to rank the results, go in the body of the request, neither stored nor logged. Your position is not part of it: the distances shown are computed on the device.

Directions and guidance

  • A route: the start (where you are), the destination, the stops (five at most), the size, weight and kind of the vehicle and its trailer, the roads to avoid and the language of the instructions go in the body of the request, without a session. Our own engine computes the route on our server; neither the request nor the route is stored, and neither our API nor the engine writes a position in its logs.
  • During guidance, on every device: the app asks for a new route from where you are, with your heading, when you leave the route, when a closure lies ahead, and when you add or remove a stop; before you confirm a stop, it asks for the route through it to show the time it adds. Each request follows the rules of the previous point.
  • Fuel along the route: when you open a route's list of stations, the app sends the line of the route as our server drew it, from its start in the preview or from its first point past the vehicle during guidance, with the fuel, the consumption and the vehicle's size. Neither the request nor the line is stored or logged.
  • Roadworks and road closures: during guidance, the app downloads the changes of the road events every three minutes. The request contains no position.
  • Danger zones and speed cameras: when a guidance starts, after each new route, then every six hours, the app asks for the data of the countries the route crosses, which it works out on the device. The request contains the list of those countries, never a position. The country you drive in, which decides what the app may show, is also worked out on the device: in France, the app shows danger zones only, never a camera's position.
  • Location during guidance: a guidance you start keeps receiving the position with the screen off or another app in front, under the permission given while using the app. The system shows it (a notification on Android, an indicator on iPhone and iPad), and everything stops at the arrival or when you leave the guidance. On a computer, the Mac and Windows apps follow the position while their window is open. In a web browser, the position comes from the browser, which asks you first, and only while the page is open; the page keeps the screen on during guidance. The instructions are spoken by the voices installed on the device; in a browser, only by those it marks as on the device, never by a voice that sends the text to its vendor.
  • Directions in another app: when you open directions in another navigation app, Lunaway gives it the destination, and for some apps your position as the start; that app handles it under its own rules.

Your account, without email

Browsing the map needs no account. The app creates one the first time you contribute (a rating, a review, a photo, a confirmation, an answer about a shop or a service, a report, a place or a vending machine, a muted author) or choose to sync your favourites. As soon as the account exists, your favourite lists are imported into it, then updated at each change.

The account rests on a key pair created on your device. To sign in, the app sends the public key, a signature and the app's language, which serves to draw the pseudonym and is not kept. Our server keeps:

  • the public key of each device linked to the account, with the dates it was created and last used, without any device name or model;
  • a randomly drawn pseudonym, such as "Curious Hedgehog of the Vercors", which you can change and which is public;
  • your sessions: a random token of which we keep only a fingerprint (SHA-256), valid for 60 days after its last use, erased within the hour after it expires;
  • the fingerprint of your recovery code (argon2id), if you created one. The code is shown only once, on your device, and we cannot recover it;
  • your trust level, from 0 to 4, computed from the account's age, the number of days it was used and the last of those days, its published contributions and the removals decided by moderators;
  • your favourite lists (their names and the places in them) and the authors you chose to mute;
  • for each contribution sent, a technical key that keeps it from being stored twice if the app sends it again: the key, the kind of operation and a fingerprint of its content, kept 30 days, or until the contribution is deleted.

If an account is banned for abuse, the reason for the ban is recorded with it.

The places, with their additions and edits, form an open database under the ODbL 1.0 licence, like OpenStreetMap, whose data it reuses. For each place addition or edit, our server also keeps a revision (its content and position) that links it to your account; it is not public.

Photos. Before sending, the app scales the photo down to 2560 pixels on the long side at most and saves it again without its metadata: no GPS position, date or camera model leaves the device. The server decodes it, then re-encodes it from the pixels alone into two WebP files (2048 and 512 pixels on the long side). The original is not kept.

What your contributions show. Your reviews and photos are public with your pseudonym, and a stable account identifier links them together. Put end to end, with their dates, they can show where you have been. A review's visit date is optional.

Moderation. Texts go through simple automatic rules (links, contact details, repetition, a word list). A text that trips them waits for a moderator before it is published. A review or photo reported by three trusted accounts is hidden until a moderator decides. Moderators, the publisher and the contributors it appoints, read the reported content and the notes attached to confirmations and reports.

You can delete each of your contributions, or your whole account: the Delete my account page details what goes and what stays.

Your location

If you allow it, the app reads the device's location while you use it, to show you on the map, compute distances, follow sunset, suggest your region and, during guidance, follow you and apply the rules of the country you drive in. These computations run on the device, which keeps only a position rounded to about ten kilometres. The app does not ask for permission to read the location in the background; only a guidance you start goes on with the screen off, as described in Directions and guidance.

Your location leaves the device in these cases:

  • as the start of a route, and of each new route of a guidance; a route's list of stations starts at its start or at the point past the vehicle, so near you. None of it is stored or logged;
  • rounded to about 5 km, for the list of the cheapest fuel around you;
  • as the position of a place or a vending machine you add: it is the point you set on the map, and after "locate me", or when the map opens on you, the map's centre is your position. It is kept with your proposal (see What you publish);
  • indirectly, through the pieces of map requested while the map is centred on you; the log keeps only their zoom level.

Confirmations, reports, place search, roadworks and danger zones contain no position. You can withdraw the permission at any time in the system settings.

Taking a place down

If a place on the map shows your home or concerns you, write to contact@lunaway.net. When we take a place down, its details, reviews, photos, confirmations and reports are deleted. So that a backup brought back into service or a new import does not bring it back, the takedown is recorded in a journal kept apart: the place's identifier, the date, a short reason (private home, GDPR, court order, other) and keyed fingerprints of the area, with no name, no position and not the text of the request. This journal is kept without a time limit and copied, encrypted, with the backups. A new place proposed near a place taken down waits for a moderator.

Logs and abuse limits

Like any web server, ours record the requests they receive, for security and troubleshooting:

  • The access log records the date, the method, the server's name, the path requested without its parameters, the response and how long it took, and the headers sent by the app or the browser (the version of the app and of the system, the processor type or the device model for the basemap, the browser and its language), with a truncated IP address: the first two numbers of an IPv4 address (203.0.113.7 becomes 203.0.0.0), the first 32 bits of an IPv6 address. It records no port, no cookies, no session tokens, no request content, not the area of map pieces, not the address of photos, and not the name of the regions downloaded. It is cut into files of 50 MB, each erased 14 days after it is closed; the server's daily images, taken by our host, keep a copy 7 days longer.
  • The servers' system journal (starts, errors) also receives the web server's lines, with addresses truncated the same way. Our API writes in it, for a request that fails, only the method and the path; the routing engine writes neither a position nor a request's content. Its files are erased once their last line is a month old, so two months at most, and the server's daily images keep them 7 days longer.
  • The abuse limits (number of requests, sign-ins, account creations, recovery code attempts) count per IP address, or per block of addresses for IPv6, in the server's memory only: they are never written down, and a restart resets them.

Backups

So that the service can be brought back after a failure, the database is copied every night, and so is each new photo:

  • on the main server for 7 days, and in that server's daily backup images at Hetzner, kept 7 days;
  • encrypted with age, on a second server at Hetzner in Nuremberg for about 15 days, then on a computer of the publisher, the only holder of the decryption key, for about 30 days. Each removes the copies that are too old during its nightly task, which the computer runs only when it is on.

Data deleted from the database therefore leaves every backup within about 30 days, longer if that computer stays off, until its next nightly task. The copy of a deleted photo leaves the main server the same night, and the two other copies after 26 days.

An account deletion is also recorded, with the account's identifier and the time, in a journal kept outside the database copies, for 45 days at most; an encrypted copy of it is made every hour and leaves every night with the backups, where it replaces the previous one (46 days at most). If a failure makes us bring a backup back into service, the account deletions made since its date are applied again before the service resumes. One failure escapes this: the loss of the server's own data disk. The deletions made after the last copy collected off that server (up to about 25 hours) would then come back with the backup.

Hosting and providers

  • Hetzner Online GmbH (Germany) hosts our servers in its data centres in Falkenstein and Nuremberg: the API, the database, the photos, the routing engine, the basemap and this site. Temporary servers at Hetzner build the road graph every week from open data, without any user data.
  • Infomaniak Network SA (Switzerland) receives the emails sent to contact@lunaway.net. Switzerland is covered by an adequacy decision of the European Commission.
  • Cloudflare runs the domain names of lunaway.net, as DNS only: it gives the address of our servers, then the traffic comes straight to us, without passing through it.
  • GitHub hosts the source code and the road graph files our server downloads. No user data goes there.

The basemap is built by Protomaps from OpenStreetMap, then copied and served by our own server. Neither the app nor this site loads any map, font or script from another host. If you install Lunaway through Google Play, F-Droid or the App Store, those services handle the download's data under their own rules.

We do not sell, rent or share any data, for advertising or for any other commercial use.

Legal bases

  • Providing the service you ask for (article 6(1)(b) of the GDPR): syncing places, directions and guidance, the search for shops and fuel, the account, your contributions, your synced favourites.
  • Our legitimate interest in protecting the service and its users (article 6(1)(f)): logs, abuse limits, moderation, backups, the deletion and takedown journals.
  • Your consent, given to the system, for reading the device's location (article 6(1)(a)): you can withdraw it at any time.

Your rights

The GDPR gives you the right to access your data, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict its use, to object to its processing and to receive it in a reusable format.

  • In the app, your profile shows your pseudonym, which you can change, your level, your devices and your contributions, which you can delete one by one.
  • You can delete your whole account in the app or with your recovery code.
  • For any other request, write to contact@lunaway.net. We answer within one month.

Your account is linked to no email address: before answering a request, we must check that the account is yours, for instance by asking you to change your pseudonym in the app to a word we give you.

If you believe your rights are not respected, you can lodge a complaint with the French data protection authority, the CNIL, online at cnil.fr or by post: CNIL, service des plaintes, 3 place de Fontenoy, 75007 Paris, France. You can also contact the authority of the EU country where you live.
